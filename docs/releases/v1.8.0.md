# Pix8 1.8.0 — the Deluxe Paint palette

Pix8 is a 256-color indexed pixel art editor built around VGA-era workflows:
one shared 256-entry palette for the whole document, independent layers,
frame animation, and a GrafX2-style palette editor.

This release changes what a new document starts with. Pix8 now opens on
**Deluxe Paint II's default palette** instead of a generated color cube —
ramps you can actually shade with. Long layer lists also get a **name filter**,
and **Shift** on the brush draws from the last pixel you drew rather than from
wherever you press.

## New: Deluxe Paint II's palette by default

The palette Pix8 shipped with until now was built by an algorithm: the 16 CGA
colors, a 6×6×6 color cube, and a 24-step gray ramp. Mathematically even, and
poor to paint with — a cube gives you every color and no ramps.

New documents now start with the 256 colors Deluxe Paint II shipped in 1994:

- the **EGA 16**,
- a **16-step gray ramp**,
- then **fourteen 16-step hue ramps** running light to dark — red, pink into
  orange, yellow, yellow-green, green, cyan, sky blue, blue, violet, magenta,
  peach, brown.

Shading is then a matter of walking up or down a column, which is the whole
point of a palette laid out by an artist rather than a loop.

**Existing files are unaffected.** Every `.pix8` stores its own palette, so
only new documents are affected; opening an older file gives you back exactly
the palette it was saved with.

The old default is still available: **`palettes/xterm-256.pal`**, loadable from
the palette editor's **Load** button. Worth noting that it was never the VGA
palette the code claimed it was — that layout (16 + 216 + 24) is the xterm-256
terminal palette, and only its middle 216 are the web-safe colors.

### Where the numbers came from

The palette was recovered from a screenshot of DPaint's own Palette requester,
then cross-checked against a second, independently-scaled rendering of the same
palette.

This matters because a palette transcribed from a lossy image is worthless —
"close to" the right 256 colors is just wrong. The check that makes it exact:
a VGA palette is 6-bit, so every channel must survive a round-trip through
6 bits. All 768 channels did, under the `(v << 2) | (v >> 4)` bit replication
the screenshot had used to widen them. Three further checks agree — indices
0–15 match the EGA 16 bit-for-bit, 16–31 are a clean gray ramp, and 11 of the
16 columns are strictly descending luminance ramps.

Four swatches sat under the mouse pointer and the selection box in that
screenshot; they were taken from the second image, and all four land exactly on
the 6-bit grid, which is independent confirmation they are right.

Indices 224–231 are black in DPaint itself — free slots, not a transcription
error.

## New: layer name filter

A filter box above the layers list narrows it to layers whose name contains
what you type, with a `12/187` match count beside it. Escape or the **×**
clears it. Matching is case-insensitive and substring-based.

It is purely a display filter — indices stay the real layer indices, so every
layer operation, multi-select and undo entry behaves exactly as before.

The **active layer stays listed even when it doesn't match**, dimmed to mark it
as a non-match. Delete, Move and Duplicate all act on the active layer, and
hiding the row those buttons operate on is how you delete something you can't
see.

## Changed: Shift draws from the last pixel you drew

Shift on the brush and eraser used to anchor its line at the point you pressed.
It now works the way GIMP does: the anchor is wherever the tool last drew, and
it outlives the stroke.

So you can put a pixel down, release, then hold **Shift** — and see a live
preview of the line running from that pixel to your cursor before you commit
it. **Ctrl** snaps that preview to 22.5° just as it does during a drag.
Committing a line re-anchors at its end, so repeated Shift+clicks chain
segments.

With no anchor yet — a freshly picked tool, or just after a document switch,
which clears it — Shift keeps the old behavior: the press point starts the line
and the drag sets its end.

## Changed: Copy is clipped to the source layer

Select All marks the whole document, so copying from a 32×32 sprite layer in a
512×512 document used to produce a 512×512 clipboard whose entire overhang was
transparent — and pasting that into a 32×32 document produced a 512×512
"Pasted" layer.

Copy and Cut now intersect the copied region with the layer's rectangle, or
with the union of visible layers' rectangles for Copy Merged. A selection that
misses the layer entirely reports **"No content to copy"** instead of silently
replacing the clipboard with a transparent block.

This clips to the layer's *rectangle*, not to its content: transparent padding
inside a sprite is usually deliberate — alignment, a hit box, a consistent cel
size — and trimming to content would silently change your sprite's dimensions.

## Changed: the palette dock sizes itself

The layers and palette docks used to split the right panel evenly. The palette
now takes exactly the height its 16 rows of swatches need, and the layers list
takes everything left over — which is most of it, and the point, since that's
the list that grows.

## Fixed in this version

- **Panel scroll positions survive a re-render.** The layers list, the frame
  timeline and the tab bar are each rebuilt from scratch, which made the
  browser clamp their scroll offset to zero — so a scrolled layers list jumped
  back to the top on *every* render, which is every brush stroke, and the frame
  timeline snapped back to frame 1 on every tick of playback. The layers list
  and frame timeline also now remember their position per tab, alongside zoom
  and pan.
- **Layer thumbnails no longer composite the whole stack on every stroke.** The
  layers panel drew a thumbnail for every layer each time it re-rendered, so
  the cost scaled with the layer count instead of with what was on screen — on
  a 187-layer document, 187 thumbnail composites per brush stroke, all but a
  dozen of them for rows scrolled out of sight. Thumbnails are now drawn only
  for rows in view, with the rest filled in as you scroll to them.
